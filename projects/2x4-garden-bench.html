Outdoor Furniture

Two-Seat 2×4 Garden Bench

A handsome, rock-solid outdoor bench built entirely from 2×4 lumber. It seats two adults comfortably, uses only straight cuts and simple screw-and-glue joinery, and can be built in a single weekend. Finished dimensions: 48" wide × 30½" deep × 36½" tall.

Dimensioned drawings

The bench is built as two identical side assemblies (left and right) that are joined by front/back rails and then topped with seat slats and back slats. Refer back to these drawings as you work through the cut list and assembly steps.

Cut list

All parts are cut from 2×4 lumber (actual 1½" × 3½"). Cut everything square (90°) unless noted. The two side assemblies are mirror images but use identical part lengths.

Tools needed

  • Miter saw or circular saw (all cuts are straight crosscuts)
  • Drill/driver + countersink bit (and a pocket-hole jig if using one)
  • Tape measure, pencil, speed square / combination square
  • Clamps (a few bar or pipe clamps make assembly far easier)
  • Random-orbit sander (or sanding block)
  • Safety glasses and hearing protection
  • Optional: ⅜" spacer scraps for even slat gaps; a jigsaw to round the armrest corners

Step-by-step assembly

  1. Cut all parts

    Work through the cut list and cut every piece (A–L) to length. Label each part with a pencil. Lightly sand mating faces with 80–120 grit now — it's much easier before assembly. If you're adding the comfort recline, cut the 5° bevels on parts A and C at this stage.

  2. Build the two side assemblies

    On a flat surface, lay out one front leg (B) and one rear post (A). Connect them with a side seat rail (C) at 17" (top of rail at seat height) and a side bottom rail (D) about 4" off the floor. Glue and screw with 3" screws. The rear post extends well above the seat to form the back. Build the second side assembly as a mirror image. Check both for square.

  3. Join the sides with the seat frame

    Stand the two side assemblies up, parallel and 45" apart (inside faces). Connect them with the front apron (F) and back seat rail (G), flush to the tops of the side seat rails. Add the two lower stretchers (H) between the bottom rails — one front, one back. Glue and screw. You now have a rigid seat frame and base.

  4. Add the seat joists

    Drop the two intermediate joists (J) into the frame, running front-to-back between the apron (F) and back rail (G), evenly spaced (roughly 15" apart — see the top-view drawing). Their tops must sit flush with the apron and side rails so the slats lie flat. Screw through the apron and back rail into each joist end (or pocket-screw from below).

  5. Install the seat slats

    Lay the five seat slats (K) across the frame, running left-to-right. Use ⅜" spacer scraps to keep the gaps even, and let a slight, equal overhang at the front. Fasten each slat to every support (side rails and both joists) with 2½" screws, two per crossing, countersunk.

  6. Attach the back slats

    Screw the four back slats (L) across the front faces of the rear posts (A), starting a couple of inches above the seat and working up with even gaps to the top of the posts. Keep them level and flush at the ends. Glue and use 2½" screws, two per post.

  7. Fit the armrests

    Set an armrest (E) on top of each front leg and rear post so it spans front-to-back. Round or chamfer the front corners for comfort, then glue and screw down into the leg and post from above (countersink and plug, or screw up from beneath the armrest for a clean top).

  8. Final sanding

    Go over the whole bench with 120 then 220 grit. Ease every edge and corner that a hand or leg will touch. Fill any countersunk holes with exterior filler and sand flush once cured.

Finishing

  1. Clean and dry

    Wipe off all dust with a dry then slightly damp cloth. Make sure the wood is dry before finishing — if you used wet pressure-treated lumber, let it dry for a few days to weeks.

  2. Apply an exterior finish

    For the warm honey tone in the photo, apply an exterior stain-sealer or penetrating oil (such as a cedar/honey-toned deck oil). Brush on with the grain, wipe back the excess, and let each coat dry per the label. Two thin coats outlast one thick coat.

  3. Protect and maintain

    Add a UV-resistant topcoat or re-oil annually for benches left in full sun and rain. Keep the feet off standing water — felt or rubber pads help on hard patios.
